# X-09 — Deadline-aware line admission (gates RUN-06)

**Result:** RESULT_READY, owner review required · **Run:** 2026-09-23 · **Code:** the BATCH_017 tree (`jit_policy: "deadline"` in `src/eval/runner.py`, off by default) · **Cost:** local only, no GPU, no LLM

## Question and criterion (decision record §4.1)

Does deadline-aware admission remove Director-caused stalls without raising latency? Monte Carlo on session A's traces plus the replay loop; slack 3.0 / 1.0 / 0.5; a Director tail with 5 % retries at 2×. **Success:** zero stalls; minimum queue margin ≥ 1 s; the latency distribution unchanged.

## Method

- `tests/jit_sim.py`: `configs/director.live.yaml` through the real live loop (`run_live`) with the mock Director and replayed 145-frame clips, 25× faster than real time; render walls and Director calls drawn from `benchmarks/runtime/baseline.json` (session A: render 4.80 ± 0.05 s, Director 2.98 ± 0.59 s, max 4.37 s), seeded per segment and attempt. A 5 % share of Director calls takes twice as long (`director_retry_rate`). The mock Director's own validator re-ask adds a second call on one scripted beat.
- Nine adversarial viewer lines per run (`scripts/experiments/admission.py`): two in the middle of a Director call, lines 0.9, 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1 s before a render fire, two 0.3 s after one, and a second line 0.4 s after the first one's answer returned. 40 clips, 10 seeds per arm.
- Arms. `current-4.7`: production (the render estimate as an EMA, `buffer_seconds` 4.7, lines admitted at once). RUN-06's candidate, `jit_policy: "deadline"`: render and Director estimates as the rolling p95 of the last 20, floored by the baseline's p95 (4.85 s, 3.80 s); a line is admitted to the clip about to fire only if the Director's p95 lands it before that fire plus τ = 1.0 s, otherwise it is answered from the clip after; buffers 3.5 / 1.5 / 1.0 s, which are the record's slack 3.0 / 1.0 / 0.5 plus the 0.5 s margin (the 1.25× surplus is gone with a p95 estimate).
- Outputs: `logs/experiments/admission/<arm>.json` (per seed: stalls, stalled turns, time held, minimum margin, every line's steering latency, deferred lines), `summary.json`.

## Results

| Arm | Stalls (runs with one) | Held | Min margin | Steering p50 / p95 / mean | Lines deferred |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| current-4.7 (production) | 6 (5/10) | 8.1 s | −2.18 s | 12.0 / 15.4 / 12.4 s | 0 |
| deadline-3.5 | **0 (0/10)** | 0 s | −0.01 s | 14.7 / 16.7 / 14.7 s | 38 |
| deadline-1.5 | 5 (5/10) | 7.4 s | −3.23 s | 12.8 / 14.7 / 12.7 s | 37 |
| deadline-1.0 | 6 (6/10) | 6.7 s | −2.51 s | 12.5 / 12.9 / 12.0 s | 30 |

No arm meets all three criteria. `deadline-3.5` removes the stalls but raises median steering latency by 2.7 s (lines it defers land a clip later) and still spends the whole margin (−0.01 s against the ≥ 1 s gate). The lower buffers stall as often as production.

**What stalls.** Tracing the stalls (seed 0 of `current-4.7`, turn 23): the stall follows a Director call at a *beat end* (no viewer line) that took 11.0 s, a validator re-ask whose draw was doubled. Admission bounds what a *line* can cost; it cannot bound the beat-end call, and that call is the tail that exceeds the buffer. Without the 5 % tail the production schedule holds with the same lines (`tests/test_jit.py`, zero stalls).

## Recommended disposition (for the owner)

- **RUN-06: do not adopt as specified.** The candidate trades stalls for latency one-for-one at 3.5 s and fails both at 1.0-1.5 s. Keep `jit_policy: "current"` and `buffer_seconds: 4.7`.
- The measured stall source is the Director's tail at beat ends (re-asks, slow calls), not line admission. The levers that address it are outside RUN-06: fewer validator re-asks, a shorter answer (ARCH-11, already in), first-clip streaming (ARCH-12 / X-19), or planning the next beat earlier than the late start when the current beat is about to end (not in the record).
- The production schedule stalled in half of these runs under a 5 % doubled-call tail. The live verification run (RUN-04 gates) will show whether the real Director has such a tail; session A's 35 calls had none above 4.37 s.
