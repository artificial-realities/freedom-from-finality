## 1. Recommended target system

### 1.1 The system

The target is the current design with its handshakes repaired, its seams closed and its prompts written in the video model's language. Nothing in the four reviews argues for replacing a layer.

**Story.** Two LLM roles on two clocks over a typed world model, unchanged in shape. The Director writes one beat per call (one action or state as one to four clips, each with its own ops) and is the only LLM call on the critical path. The Showrunner is memory (compaction, notes, thread pruning) and the author of the idle film (arc, scene card, candidate beats); it never blocks a segment. The authority rule, today implicit, becomes explicit: while a viewer direction is active the Director outranks the card, and the card is a menu for the quiet stretches. Four handshake defects get deterministic fixes: a held replan is remembered, the Showrunner's snapshot is taken after the triggering beat's first clip is applied, a second viewer line arriving in the slack window is joined, and a deferred request is resolved by identity. Whether viewer momentum earns its place and whether candidates should be hidden while a direction is active are experiments, not code changes.

**Duration.** One runtime object owns the clip shape, in frames: render frames, overlap frames, delivered frames. The Director is told the delivered length and writes about that much action; the compiler has no duration; the model's duration head stays off; one shape is warmed and the client refuses to start on a shape the server has not warmed. `i2v_duration` goes.

**Picture.** LTX-2.5 distilled, eight sigmas, unguided, continuity strength 1.0, a nine-frame window (1 or 17 pending measurement), anchor off, no enhancer, no LoRA, no multi-shot, a seed derived per session and segment. The negative prompt leaves the live config because the distilled path never encodes it. The compiler emits one paragraph of caption prose: a style clause, the opening state from the world model (posture, position, held and worn items), the framing triple as prose, the action in chronological sentences with the sound woven in, entering entities introduced inside the action, dialogue in the official form, "the shot ends with …", and the narrator as an unseen voice. The blueprint's style becomes positive caption prose and `look_short` names only invariants. The Director prompt carries three generic renderer rules: plausible motion, no internal states without a visible cue, about a clip's worth of action.

**Seams.** The overlap stays in the file. The server pads audio to the video and applies a loudness ceiling and a true-peak limit at encode. The player starts clip N+1 when clip N reaches its frame `n − overlap`, switches the picture without an opacity fade (the frames are the same), and crossfades the audio equal-power over the overlap with a speech-safe variant. The hold is the delivered length minus the overlap. Two `<video>` elements with an element pool and a media-time start are the first implementation; a single MSE timeline only if the seam gates are still missed.

**Audio.** LTX owns everything inside a clip: ambience, foley, dialogue and, by default, narration (measured: 15 of 15 lines rendered verbatim and fitted to the clip). The player owns everything between clips. Audio spanning, the serial render path and `audio_seconds_owed` go. TTS survives only if a listening test says the LTX narrator is not one voice; if it survives it is rebuilt as a parallel, never-spanning path. Whether a film has a narrator, its voice, language and silence floor move to the blueprint; `narration` stays a rendering choice, which restores the rule that rendering flags never gate content. One voice per clip and about two spoken words per second of clip.

**Scheduling.** Just-in-time rendering stays. `lead_clips` is renamed to what it does (startup only) and the steady-state buffer becomes an explicit number. Lines are admitted against a deadline computed from the Director's p95 so a late line cannot spend the buffer; estimates become quantiles; the slack shrinks afterwards. A failed render rolls the world back to the last picture the viewer saw and retries when time allows. A server restart restores the window from the last delivered clip instead of the reference.

**Measurement.** Every run writes a timing log and browser beacons; a machine-readable baseline with tail gates (render p95 ≤ 0.85×, max ≤ 0.92×, zero stalls, minimum margin ≥ 1 s) replaces the four "×RT" figures in the notes; an offline JIT simulation test, a headless seam check, an audio check and a compiler replay harness let agents catch regressions without a GPU.

### 1.2 Why this balance

*Continuity* comes from strength 1.0, the overlap kept, no re-seed mid-film and the world model's state in every prompt. *Steerability* comes from the beat being cut on input, the just-in-time fire and deadline-aware admission; nothing here lengthens the line-to-screen path, and two levers (answer beats of at most two clips, first-clip streaming) shorten it. *Latency* is protected by tail gates, one warmed shape, one render path and the Director as the only call on the critical path. *Quality* comes from prompts in the model's dialect, a plausible-motion rule and a loudness ceiling. *Maintainability* comes from one owner per fact: one shape object, one render path, one narration source, one authority rule, and dead code removed.

## 6. Questions for the project owner

Each answer changes the design. A recommended option is given for each.

1. **Does the Lego film have a narrator, and how often does it speak?** The blueprint says "no voiceover" in constraint 4 and defines an Attenborough narrator in the tone guidelines; the system follows the latter (16 lines in 64 clips, one every four to five clips, and the Director fills nearly every open gate). *Recommended:* yes; delete "no voiceover" from constraint 4; add a `narrator` object (AUD-06) with the 15 s floor; revisit density after the next run.
**Owner answer:** Every story should define a narrator and its tone, voice, language, and story-specific guidance. However, narration should not follow a universal frequency or fixed schedule. The multi-agent system—primarily the Director when constructing each beat—should decide contextually whether a moment is best served by narration, character dialogue, or silence. The result should resemble a traditional film, where narration is used when it adds value and dialogue is used when it fits the scene. The implementation must remain story-agnostic: narrator behaviour and guidance belong in each story blueprint rather than being hard-coded globally.
2. **Should a viewer's direction outrank a scene card written after it, and how long should it last?** Today a landing card erases momentum and stale candidates pull the film back. *Recommended:* the direction outranks the card until it ages out (about 90 story seconds) or the next line; candidates hidden while it is active; confirm with X-07 and X-08 before the code changes (ARCH-08, ARCH-09).
**Owner answer:** A viewer’s direction should temporarily outrank the current scene card. It should continue shaping the story until it is replaced by a new viewer direction or naturally expires after approximately 90 seconds of story time. While the direction remains active, scene-card candidate beats should not pull the film back toward the previous plan. The exact expiry, reset behaviour, and handling of candidate beats should be validated through experiments X-07 and X-08 before the production behaviour is changed.
3. **Fail-stop or fail-soft?** An exception in `emit` ends the film; a failed render leaves the world one clip ahead of the picture. *Recommended:* fail-soft with rollback and a bounded retry (RUN-09).
**Owner answer:** Use fail-soft handling. If a clip fails to render, retry it once only when sufficient buffer time remains. If the retry is unsafe or also fails, restore the world and beat state to the point before the failed clip, discard the failed continuation, and replan from the last clip the viewer successfully received. A failed generation should not end the film or leave the internal story ahead of what the viewer has actually seen. All failures and recovery actions should be logged, and retry loops must be prevented.
4. **"A consistent documentary voice" or "this exact voice"?** LTX gives the first at no cost; the second needs TTS and a mux path. *Recommended:* LTX; run X-01; delete `src/tts/` and the mux module if it passes (AUD-05).
**Owner answer:** Use native LTX narration by default. Each story should define the narrator’s desired tone, language, accent, and delivery, while LTX generates the narration together with the video and other scene audio. Run experiment X-01 to determine whether listeners perceive the LTX narrator as sufficiently consistent across clips. If it passes, remove the current external TTS pipeline. If it fails, retain or redesign external TTS as an optional rendering mode for stories that require an exact and recognizable voice identity.
5. **Music and room tone?** Neither is needed for the seams. *Recommended:* no music in the MVP (AUD-08); room tone only if X-02 leaves seams audible (AUD-09).
**Owner answer:** Do not build a dedicated background-music system for the current MVP. LTX should continue generating the scene’s native ambience, foley, dialogue, and narration. The architecture should remain story-agnostic so individual stories can later define whether music is desired and provide story-specific musical guidance. If music is added later, it should be generated and managed at the scene level rather than independently for every clip. Do not add room tone yet. First improve and test the clip-to-clip audio transitions; add an optional story-specific room-tone layer only if experiment X-02 shows that audible silence gaps remain.
6. **Schema and blueprint-model changes.** `BeatPlan.delivered_request` (ARCH-07), `Story.narrator` (AUD-06), `Character.voice` (LTX-05). *Recommended:* approve all three; each is inside the strict subset or a blueprint field, and each closes a verified gap.
**Owner answer:** Approve all three schema additions. `BeatPlan.delivered_request` should identify the specific deferred viewer request fulfilled by a beat, using the project’s existing stable intervention identifier. `Story.narrator` should define each story’s narrator, including tone, voice characteristics, language, accent, and story-specific usage guidance. `Character.voice` should define how speaking characters sound, including relevant language, accent, delivery, and vocal characteristics. These fields should remain story-agnostic and should not hard-code behaviour for the current Lego story.
7. **The creature hole in the cast lock.** A brick-built princess that gets kissed and crowned is a prop to the validator and a second figure to the viewer. *Recommended:* accept it and say so in the blueprint ("creature props never have minifigure parts"), which also gives the renderer the words that would have prevented turn 35's tuxedoed figure.
**Owner answer:** Replace the universal cast lock with a protagonist-preservation and story-configurable cast policy. Each story blueprint should define its main protagonist, including stable identity, appearance, personality, voice, starting state, and characteristics that must not drift. The blueprint should separately define the default camera perspective—such as first-person, third-person, observer, or permitted switching rules.

A story may define specific supporting characters that must, may, or should appear under particular narrative conditions. It may also allow dynamically generated characters and provide guidance describing what those characters should be like. If this guidance is left open, the multi-agent system may decide creatively within the story’s general constraints.

The Showrunner should decide when introducing a supporting character serves the story, while the Director stages the introduction and controls the character’s immediate actions. Once introduced, every new character must be registered in the world model with a stable identity, appearance, voice, role, speaking permission, and state so that it remains consistent. The validator should protect protagonist continuity and enforce the individual story’s cast and perspective policy rather than globally prohibiting new characters.
8. **GPU authorisation.** Two box sessions (about five hours in total, roughly $13 at 03's rate plus LLM calls) cover every blocking experiment. *Recommended:* authorise both, the first for X-03 to X-06 and the second for X-10 to X-12 and the verification run.
**Owner answer:** Authorize the two proposed GPU experiment sessions with a total GPU rental budget cap of $30, excluding ordinary LLM usage. All relevant local tests, experiment harnesses, configurations, and comparison inputs must be prepared before a GPU is rented. Related experiments should reuse the same warmed server whenever practical. Every run must record its hardware, code revision, configuration, seeds, prompts, stage timings, and outputs. The GPU instance must be terminated immediately after the planned work finishes. Stop and request additional approval before exceeding the budget, materially expanding the experiments, or continuing after repeated infrastructure failures.
